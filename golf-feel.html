01

Two ways to pay attention

Attention aimed at the body versus attention aimed at the club. Research on motor learning (Gabriele Wulf's work, among others) finds external focus reliably produces better strikes and better retention than internal focus — the effect holds for scratch golfers, not just beginners. But both have a job. The mistake is using the wrong one at the wrong time.

Internal

For learning · slow · off ball
"Shift to the left heel."
"Keep the head still."

Attention on body parts. You cannot build a movement you cannot feel, so this is how a pattern gets installed.

  • Drill work, no ball
  • Half speed or slower
  • No target, no score
  • Never on the course

External

For playing · full speed · at a target
"Follow the clubface."
"Throw the paint."

Attention on the club, the ball, or the target. Lets the pattern run without interference, which is where the speed and the strike quality live.

  • Every ball struck at a target
  • All eighteen holes
  • Every pre-shot rehearsal
  • The back half of every range session
The actual failure isn't the drill — it's the missing handoff. A session that ends on internal cues sends you to the first tee still running the checklist. Every practice block needs a deliberate switch back before it counts as finished.

Before the range

    04

    Translate the cue

    Every drill cue has an external twin that produces the same motion without the body-checking. Learn the left-hand side on the range. Take only the right-hand side toward the course. Tap a card to turn it over.

    Finding the head

    06

    Pick one

    Everything above is raw material. This is what actually goes to the course — one thought, held all round. Sports psychologist Bob Rotella's advice points the same way: once you're set up over the ball, the mind should be quiet except for this.

      07

      The ritual

      Same five steps on every full shot, every round. The routine is what carries the external focus onto the course — it is the delivery mechanism, not a superstition.

        Hold the thought all round. Swapping mid-round is how three bad holes become nine. If it stops working, finish the round on it anyway and change it next time out, not mid-way through this one.
        08

        When it falls apart

        This will happen. The goal is not preventing it — it is catching it by the second bad shot instead of the sixth.

          Alignment stick — two different drills, same tool. Aimed at the hips at setup, it's a pre-shot check. Held against the shaft during a slow wind-up, it's Danny Maude's plane-feedback drill above. Neither is something to think about mid-swing.
